# Global Coffee Connect

LeAlvin Global — Company & Website Direction
1. The company behind the brand

The legal company presented in the profile is Le Alvin Enterprises Limited, operating under the Le-Alvin Coffee identity. The profile describes the company as registered under Kenya's Companies Act and licensed through the coffee regulatory framework to operate as a coffee dealer, including sourcing coffee through local auctions and handling exports and imports.

This isn't simply a coffee shop or local coffee seller.

The company positions itself as an international coffee trading, sourcing, processing, packaging and export business.

That is also consistent with external records: Le-Alvin Enterprises Ltd appears in Agriculture and Food Authority coffee-dealer records, and historical Kenya Gazette records identify the company as a coffee-industry business.

The profile identifies:

CEO / Managing Director: Margaret Njeri

And the profile's contact page gives:

Telephone: +254 722 152 087 / +254 722 646 290
Office: 0783 180 770
Email: info@lealvins.com
Website: www.lealvins.com

So the new website can be branded LeAlvin Global, while the existing corporate identity/domain remains Le-Alvin / lealvins.com unless Margaret decides to change the domain as part of the rebrand.

2. What LeAlvin actually does

The company profile gives you several distinct business functions that should become website sections.

Coffee sourcing

Le-Alvin sources coffee locally through the Kenyan coffee auction system and also operates its own farm, Maggie Estate.

At Maggie Estate, the company says it:

grows coffee
harvests it
processes it
markets its own produce

The company also has an in-house professional responsible for tasting samples and maintaining quality standards.

This is excellent website material because it gives you a farm-to-global-market story.

International coffee trading

The company specializes in exporting Kenyan original Arabica coffee around the world.

It also imports Arabica coffee from:

Burundi
Uganda
Tanzania
Ethiopia

and exports according to customer requirements.

External trade records also show Le-Alvin associated with international coffee shipments, including coffee beans and Uganda-origin coffee.

So the website should communicate:

Kenyan origin + East African sourcing + international market access.

That is much stronger than simply saying "we sell coffee."

11. Who the website should be designed for

This is important.

I would not design LeAlvin Global primarily like a normal retail coffee website.

The profile suggests several audiences:

International coffee buyers

Companies looking for Kenyan/East African coffee.

Coffee importers

Businesses sourcing green coffee internationally.

Roasters

Businesses looking for green beans for their own roasting operations.

Cafés / hospitality businesses

Potential customers looking for roasted or supplied coffee.

Distributors

Companies requiring larger quantities.

Private-label businesses

Companies wanting their own branding and packaging.

Specialty coffee buyers

Customers looking for specific grades and quality.

Therefore, the website needs a strong B2B / international trade feel.

22. The visual identity I'd recommend

For LeAlvin Global, I would avoid making it look like a generic brown coffee website.

Instead:

Style

Premium + African + International + Agricultural + Modern

Think:

deep coffee tones
warm earth tones
cream
subtle gold
natural greens

But importantly, don't make the African identity look stereotypical or rustic/poor.

The company is presenting itself as an international exporter, so the African identity should feel premium, sophisticated and authentic.

23. The website's core message

If I were defining the entire website in one sentence:

LeAlvin Global is a premium East African coffee company connecting responsibly sourced African coffee with customers around the world through sourcing, processing, quality control, roasting, packaging and international export.

That's the story the website should tell.

And the company's existing slogan gives you an excellent brand anchor:

Coffee Is Us. Quality Is Us.

One thing I would clarify with Margaret before you build

There is a difference between the company profile and the new brand direction.

The PDF is specifically branded Le Alvin Coffee / Le Alvin Enterprises Limited, while Margaret has now told you that she wants the new website to be called LeAlvin Global.

So before you start changing the branding, I would establish whether:

LeAlvin Global = a new brand under Le-Alvin Enterprises Ltd

or

LeAlvin Global = a complete rebrand of Le-Alvin Coffee

or

LeAlvin Global = the parent/international brand, with Le-Alvin Coffee remaining one of its brands.

The third option could actually be very powerful if the company is genuinely expanding beyond coffee.

For example:

LEALVIN GLOBAL
Global Trade. African Origin.

Our Businesses

LeAlvin Coffee
Tea
Spices
Agricultural Commodities
International Trading

But tea, spices and those additional divisions should only be placed on the site after Margaret confirms that they are actually part of the company's business, because the PDF you provided doesn't establish them.

The existing domain is also significant: the company profile itself lists www.lealvins.com and info@lealvins.com, so I would build the new site around that existing corporate identity unless Margaret tells you they're changing domains.

And externally, the company has a documented history as a Kenyan coffee dealer, including current AFA coffee-dealer records and Kenya Gazette records.

In short: you're not being asked to build "just a website." You're being given the opportunity to build LeAlvin's digital international storefront and potentially its new global brand identity. That's why I'd approach the project as a premium B2B international trade website, with the coffee story at its center and room for the company to expand into other product categories once Margaret confirms them.
